Inside the bot's brain

Like Renaissance Technologies, the bot doesn't rely on one big idea. It combines 8 small statistical signals and learns from recent data how much to trust each one. Each signal starts from what 8 years of NEPSE research found (the ▼ marker), then adjusts to how well it worked over the last year.

How it works, step by step

  1. 3:00 PM: market closes. A GitHub Actions robot wakes up and downloads every stock's open, high, low, close, volume and average price (VWAP), plus the NEPSE index. Holidays are skipped automatically.
  2. Filter. It drops mutual funds, bonds, debentures, promoter shares and the least-traded 40% of stocks, so every trade is realistic for a Rs 5 lakh account.
  3. 8 signals. Chosen from 8 years of NEPSE research: closing pressure, low volatility, no lottery spikes, short-term bounce, near 52-week high, circuit chasing, momentum and size.
  4. Learn. Each signal's trust = 50% what 8 years of research says + 50% how well it predicted the next 20 days over the last year (only past data, never the future).
  5. Never-buy rules. No stock that hit the upper circuit today, hit the lower circuit in the last 5 days, or has the heaviest late-day closing pressure. These traps were the most reliable patterns in the research.
  6. Review every 40 trading days. Holds about 10–12 stocks (max 12% each, 35% per sector). It buys from the top 15% and keeps holdings while they stay in the top 35%. Trading less keeps NEPSE's fees and taxes from eating the returns.
  7. Safety exits, checked daily. −15% from entry, −22% from peak, or a lower-circuit day. Money in stocks is cut to 60% only in a confirmed bear market (NEPSE below a falling 200-day average).
  8. Next session & T+2. Orders fill at that day's VWAP + 0.25% slippage, and fail if the stock is locked at a circuit. Bought shares can't be sold until they settle 2 trading days later.
VWAP

Volume-weighted average price: the average price everyone paid that day.

T+2 settlement

Shares bought today reach your demat account 2 trading days later. No intraday trading in NEPSE.

Circuit breaker

A stock can move at most ±10% per day. At the limit, nobody may be willing to trade.

Stop-loss

An automatic exit when a trade loses a set amount, to stop small losses becoming big ones.

Drawdown

The fall from the highest value to the lowest point after it.

Sharpe ratio

Return per unit of risk. Above 1 is good; above 2 is excellent.
