Inside the bot's brain

Like Renaissance Technologies, the bot doesn't rely on one big idea. It combines 7 small statistical signals, almost all describing the same thing from different angles: how much crowd attention a stock is getting. On NEPSE the crowd's favourites lag, and quiet, steady stocks lead. The weights are fixed from 8 years of research; in testing, re-learning them every day only added noise.

How it works, step by step

  1. After 3:10 PM: market closed. A GitHub Actions robot downloads every stock's prices, volume and average price (VWAP), plus the NEPSE index. It refuses half-day or incomplete data, and holidays are skipped automatically.
  2. Filter. It drops mutual funds, bonds, debentures, promoter shares and the least-traded 40% of stocks, so every trade is realistic for a Rs 1–5 lakh account.
  3. Score. 7 signals measure crowd attention: how heavily a stock trades, whether its turnover just surged, late-day price pushes, circuit chasing, lottery-like spikes, plus calmness and distance from the 52-week high. Quiet, steady stocks score best.
  4. Never-buy rules. No stock at the upper circuit today, none that hit the lower circuit in the last 5 days, none within 20 days of a bonus/right-share price adjustment, and none of the 10% with the heaviest late-day buying.
  5. Build the portfolio. Rs 5 lakh: 16 stocks of about 6% each. Rs 1 lakh: 10 stocks of about Rs 10,000 each, because smaller slices would fall under a sensible minimum order and fixed fees would bite. Max 35% per sector, bought from the top 15% of scores. Always almost fully invested: trying to time the market did not help in testing.
  6. Review each holding every 60 trading days. A stock is kept while it stays in the better half of all scores, otherwise replaced. Reviews are staggered so the whole portfolio is never re-decided on one day.
  7. No stop-losses. In 8 years of data, stocks sold by a stop-loss did not keep falling, so stops only locked in losses and doubled the fees. The one automatic exit is a lower-circuit day.
  8. Realistic execution. Orders fill at the next session's VWAP plus a cost that grows with order size and how thin the stock is. Locked circuits, T+2 settlement, suspensions, commission, SEBON fee, DP charge and capital-gains tax are all simulated.
VWAP

Volume-weighted average price: the average price everyone paid that day.

T+2 settlement

Shares bought today reach your demat account 2 trading days later. No intraday trading in NEPSE.

Circuit breaker

A stock can move at most ±10% per day. At the limit, nobody may be willing to trade.

Stop-loss

An automatic exit when a trade loses a set amount, to stop small losses becoming big ones.

Drawdown

The fall from the highest value to the lowest point after it.

Sharpe ratio

Return per unit of risk. Above 1 is good; above 2 is excellent.
